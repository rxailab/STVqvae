\begin{abstract}
Linear probes are a standard diagnostic for evaluating learned representations: if simple classifiers can recover semantic labels from a latent, the representation is often deemed meaningful. We show that this criterion can fail to predict the representation's usability inside a jointly trained world model. We study three VQ-VAE variants (baseline, dead-code restart, goal-aware) and one continuous spatial VAE, trained end-to-end with PPO on MiniGrid DoorKey-8$\times$8 (2--5 seeds per encoder; 23 checkpoints total) and DoorKey-16$\times$16 (3 seeds, v6), and compare two notions of representation quality: per-class linear-probe recall on the per-cell latent features, and per-class world-model accuracy under $k$-step free-running rollouts ($k \in \{1, 3, 5, 10\}$). Probe recall is high on \texttt{door} (0.72--0.97) and \texttt{goal} (0.87--0.98) and moderate on \texttt{key} (0.50--0.67) and \texttt{agent} (0.50--0.70), while per-class WM accuracy remains below 0.2 on every spatially non-stationary class (\texttt{door}, \texttt{key}, \texttt{agent}) in every encoder; in the continuous VAE, probe accuracy reaches 0.97 on \texttt{door} while WM accuracy is $\le 0.02$ at every horizon. The single exception is \texttt{goal}, which is spatially constant in DoorKey and which the WM memorizes as such in two VQ variants. Pooling all 26 valid checkpoints, the across-class Pearson correlation between probe and WM accuracy is $+0.11$ [95\% bootstrap CI $-0.04$, $+0.24$] at $k{=}1$ and $+0.02$ [$-0.12$, $+0.15$] at $k{=}10$---intervals that include zero at every horizon and exclude the $\rho \ge 0.8$ regime that a usable proxy would require. The continuous VAE shows the gap is not reducible to quantization, indicating that codebook-level effects are at most a partial mechanism. Our results argue that probe accuracy is necessary but not sufficient for representation usability in MBRL, and we recommend reporting per-class $k$-step WM accuracy as a cheap drop-in companion to probes.
\end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}

  Learned-latent world models have become a dominant paradigm for model-based reinforcement learning \citep{ha2018worldmodels,moerland2023mbrl}. Systems like Dreamer \citep{hafner2023diverse}, IRIS \citep{micheli2023iris}, and DIAMOND \citep{alonso2024diamond} train an encoder---often a VQ-VAE \citep{oord2017vqvae}---end-to-end or jointly with a downstream transition model, assuming that the latent produced by the encoder faithfully captures the environment's structure for the WM to use. When the quality of these representations is evaluated at all, the standard tool is a linear probe \citep{alain2016probes}: a per-class logistic regression over the per-cell latent features, whose accuracy is reported as evidence that the encoder has ``grounded'' each semantic class it encounters. High probe accuracy is then taken as license to deploy the representation downstream---in a transition model, a policy, or a planner.

  We show that this protocol can fail in a way that matters. In MiniGrid DoorKey, a benchmark where objects are few, distinct, and reliably labelable, an encoder trained end-to-end with PPO \textbf{recovers \texttt{door} and \texttt{goal} with probe recall above 0.87}, and \texttt{key} and \texttt{agent} at moderate probe recall (0.50--0.70)---and in the continuous-latent case, above 0.97 on \texttt{door} and \texttt{goal}---while the concurrently-trained transition model predicts \texttt{door}, \texttt{key}, and \texttt{agent} correctly only 0--20\% of the time, even at one step of free-running rollout. The gap is largest where the probe is strongest: probe-high, WM-low on \texttt{door}; probe-moderate, WM-low on \texttt{key} and \texttt{agent}. The pattern holds across every encoder family we study and survives at horizons $k \in \{1, 5, 10\}$. One class---\texttt{goal}---is a partial exception in two VQ variants: the DoorKey goal is in a fixed grid corner, and the WM can attain high accuracy on it by memorizing a constant. The remaining classes show the pattern uniformly.

  Across seeds, encoders, and horizons, the per-seed across-class Pearson correlation between probe accuracy and world-model accuracy is near zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:scatter},~\ref{fig:pearson}). No encoder family we tested yields a confidently positive correlation; the continuous VAE's correlation at $k{=}10$ is \emph{negative}, driven by three of five seeds in which WM accuracy worsens exactly on the classes the probe most confidently recovers. Concurrent work \citep{zhang2026probingwm} probes IRIS and DIAMOND with causal interventions and reports a similar concern that internal representations of trained world models do not match the picture probe accuracy alone would suggest; we view that contribution as complementary to the constructive across-class evidence we provide here.

  A natural first hypothesis is that the gap is a property of vector quantization---codebook collapse, the EMA update's bias toward high-frequency classes, and the auxiliary cross-entropy all interact in ways that could plausibly hurt forward prediction while leaving the probe intact. Our evidence complicates this story: the same gap appears---at larger magnitude---in a continuous spatial VAE that removes quantization entirely while sharing the backbone, the decoder, and the transition-model architecture with the VQ encoders. Codebook effects may explain part of the discrete case, but they are not the whole story; the dissociation survives when no codebook exists. Within the discrete regime we also find a finer-grained dissociation, discussed in \S4.4, between probe-level and WM-level failure modes.

  Our contribution is threefold. First, we formalize per-class world-model accuracy as a direct counterpart to per-class probe accuracy, computed via free-running $k$-step rollouts with action sequences matched to ground-truth environment transitions. This lets us compare what probes say about a representation to what the world model can do with it, along the same axis. Second, we show that these two metrics are empirically decoupled across VQ variants and a continuous VAE in a setting where probes are most commonly used---leading to an actionable recommendation: probe accuracy is necessary but not sufficient evidence for downstream usability in model-based RL. Third, we provide partial mechanistic evidence that \emph{codebook effects alone do not explain the gap}: dead-code restart does not close it, and the gap persists in the continuous case. We propose joint probe + $k$-step WM evaluation, trivial to compute, as the default representation-quality metric whenever the intended downstream use is a transition model.

  \section{Related Work}

  \paragraph{Discrete and continuous world models for MBRL.}
  Learning compact latent dynamics from pixels has become central to sample-efficient RL \citep{ha2018worldmodels,moerland2023mbrl}. The Dreamer family \citep{hafner2019dream,hafner2020atari,hafner2023diverse} popularized end-to-end joint encoder + transition learning. Subsequent systems---IRIS \citep{micheli2023iris}, $\Delta$-IRIS \citep{micheli2024deltairis}, STORM \citep{zhang2023storm}, DRAMA \citep{wang2025drama}, DIAMOND \citep{alonso2024diamond}, EfficientZero V2 \citep{wang2024efficientzerov2}, Genie \citep{bruce2024genie}, hierarchical discrete-latent THICK \citep{gumbsch2024thick}, and MuZero-descendants \citep{schrittwieser2020muzero}---shifted toward discrete tokens (VQ-VAE / VQ-GAN), while continuous-latent MBRL continues to scale through TD-MPC2 \citep{hansen2024tdmpc2}. We train the same class of encoder end-to-end with PPO and ask whether the tokens are actually \emph{useful} to the transition model that consumes them.

  \paragraph{VQ-VAE and codebook collapse.}
  Vector quantization \citep{oord2017vqvae} suffers from codebook collapse; mitigations include EMA updates \citep{razavi2019vqvae2}, dead-code restart \citep{yu2021vqgan,huh2023straightening}, the rotation-trick reparameterization \citep{fifty2025rotation}, and the quantization-free FSQ alternative \citep{mentzer2024fsq}. These are evaluated on reconstruction and codebook utilization, not on downstream WM usability. We find that dead-code restart recovers rare-class probe accuracy without recovering per-class WM accuracy---separating the ``recovered-codebook'' story from the ``usable-representation'' story. Encoder-side regularization for world models \citep{poudel2024recore,wang2025stepvq} targets the encoder rather than the WM and is orthogonal to our finding.

  \paragraph{Probing and prior critiques.}
  Linear probes \citep{alain2016probes} are the default diagnostic for whether a representation captures a target concept; methodology has been refined through control tasks \citep{hewitt2019control}, MDL probing \citep{voita2020mdl}, information-theoretic formulations \citep{pimentel2020info}, and surveyed in \citet{belinkov2022probing}, with NLP/vision evidence linking probe accuracy to downstream transfer \citep{conneau2018cram,kornblith2019similarity}. A growing strand questions whether probe accuracy actually predicts downstream behavior: \citet{elazar2021amnesic} ablate decodable information and find it causally irrelevant; \citet{vafa2024evaluating} formalise world-model recovery via Myhill-Nerode metrics and show probe-passable transformers can hide incoherent dynamics; \citet{zhang2026probingwm} causally probes IRIS and DIAMOND with the same conclusion. We extend this line by characterising a regime---learned-latent MBRL with MLP transition models---where probe accuracy reflects a real decodable signal yet is non-predictive of downstream WM accuracy, and we offer constructive (not ablative) evidence of the gap on the same axis as the probe.

  \paragraph{Object-centric and decision-relevant representations.}
  Object-centric work \citep{locatello2020slot,greff2020binding,singh2022steve} matches representations to ground-truth scene structure; we measure how well the representation \emph{survives} the downstream transition model instead. A parallel theoretical line has long argued model quality should be judged by the decisions a model supports: value-aware model learning \citep{farahmand2018ivaml}, DeepMDP \citep{gelada2019deepmdp}, and recent value-aligned regularizers \citep{jiang2025vawmreg,valliappan2026multiscale}. Our per-class $k$-step WM accuracy is a measurement-side instrument for that intuition.

  \section{Method}

This section formalizes the setup evaluated in \S4. We first describe the joint encoder--policy--world-model architecture (\S3.1), then define the two instruments whose disagreement is the subject of the paper: the \emph{semantic probe} (\S3.2) and \emph{$k$-step world-model accuracy} (\S3.3). Section 3.4 defines the across-class correlation we report in \S4.5, and \S3.5 introduces the one architectural novelty required for the continuous-latent comparison---a spatial variant of the continuous VAE that preserves per-token geometry so that per-class WM accuracy is well-defined without quantization.

\subsection{Setup and architecture}

We consider a partially observed MDP with pixel observations $o_t$ and a discrete action set $\mathcal{A}$. A MiniGrid observation $o_t$ is an $H \times W \times 3$ image whose ground-truth spatial class grid $y_t \in \mathcal{C}^{H' \times W'}$ takes values from an 11-class vocabulary (empty, wall, door, key, goal, agent, \ldots); these labels are used only for evaluation. An agent learns three modules jointly: a convolutional encoder $\phi_\omega : \mathcal{O} \to \mathbb{R}^{D \times H' \times W'}$ producing a per-cell latent map (followed by a VQ quantizer $q_\psi$ in the discrete setting and per-cell Gaussian heads in the continuous setting); a transition model $f_\theta$, a flat MLP that consumes flattened code indices (VQ) or flattened continuous features (VAE) and predicts the next latent; and a PPO policy $\pi_\eta$ on the flattened post-bottleneck features with end-to-end gradients via the straight-through estimator. The training objective combines PPO, reconstruction (pixel MSE plus VAE KL), the transition trainer's online one-step loss, and a discrete-only auxiliary cross-entropy on next-step code indices with weight $\alpha_{\text{aux}}$ (set to $0$ for the VAE since the cross-entropy is undefined there). All modules are trained jointly and on-policy; the world model is learning the dynamics of a non-stationary distribution of behavior. Per-module input choices and the full loss decomposition are summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:arch-detail}.

\subsection{Semantic probes}

For every frozen checkpoint we fit a multinomial logistic probe on the per-token continuous embedding $\tilde\phi(o)_{:,i,j} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (the post-quantization continuous feature for VQ; the per-cell posterior mean $\mu(o)$ for the VAE). The probe is trained on $N_{\text{probe}}{=}20{,}000$ random-action frames, contributing $H'W'$ per-cell samples each, with class-balanced cross-entropy ($C{=}1.0$, 300 iterations). We report per-class \textbf{recall on the training set}: the fraction of cells of true class $c$ that the probe assigns to $c$. With $O(10^3)$ probe parameters on $O(10^6)$ samples the generalization gap is negligible; using the same random-action distribution as the world-model evaluation in \S3.3 keeps both instruments on matched states. Full protocol details are in Appendix~\ref{app:probe-protocol}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$k$-step}{k-step} world-model accuracy}

The world-model analogue of the probe is the accuracy with which the transition model \emph{propagates} class information forward under free-running rollout. We collect $R{=}500$ random-action rollouts of length up to $k_{\max}{=}10$ and roll the WM free-running from each starting latent: $\hat\ell_0 = \ell^\star_0$, $\hat\ell_{t+1} = \mathrm{decode}(f_\theta(\mathrm{vec}(\hat\ell_t), a_t))$ with the actual environment action $a_t$, where \texttt{decode} is argmax over code logits (VQ) or identity (VAE). A per-cell prediction is correct iff the predicted code index equals the true index at that cell (VQ), or, for the VAE, iff among all $H'W'$ cells in the true latent grid the cosine-nearest to $\hat\ell_{k,ij}$ is exactly $(i,j)$ --- a criterion strictly stronger than feature MSE and well-defined without a post-hoc codebook. \textbf{Per-class $k$-step WM accuracy} is then
\begin{equation}
\text{WM}_c^{(k)} \;=\; \frac{\sum_{r,i,j} \mathbb{1}[y_{0,ij,r}=c] \cdot \mathrm{Match}_{ij}(\hat\ell_{k,r}, \ell^\star_{k,r})}{\sum_{r,i,j} \mathbb{1}[y_{0,ij,r}=c]},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ ``of all cells that started as class $c$, what fraction does the WM still predict correctly $k$ steps later?''. Bucketing alternatives, the choice of free-running over teacher-forcing, and full implementation details are in Appendix~\ref{app:wm-protocol}.

\subsection{Probe--WM correlation}

We summarise the relationship on a single checkpoint with the per-seed across-class Pearson correlation $\rho^{(k)}_s = \mathrm{corr}(\{\text{Probe}_c\}, \{\text{WM}_c^{(k)}\})_s$ over the five task-critical classes $\mathcal{C}^{\star} = \{\texttt{wall},\texttt{door},\texttt{key},\texttt{goal},\texttt{agent}\}$, and aggregate across seeds via nonparametric bootstrap. Under the null that probe accuracy is a reliable proxy, $\mathbb{E}_s[\rho^{(1)}_s]$ should be $\gtrsim 0.8$ and decay gradually with $k$.

\subsection{AEModelSpatial: a continuous-latent comparison}

The standard continuous VAE used in prior MBRL work (\texttt{AEModel}) collapses the full $(D, H', W')$ feature map into a single flat $D$-dimensional latent vector, which makes per-cell WM accuracy undefined. We therefore introduce \texttt{AEModelSpatial}, a drop-in replacement that applies two $1{\times}1$ convolutions to produce per-cell mean and log-variance maps $\mu(o), \log\sigma(o) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times H' \times W'}$, preserves the latent grid for the probe and WM, and is otherwise identical to v6 in backbone, decoder, and transition MLP. Trained under a standard $\beta$-VAE objective with $\beta$ matched to the VQ commitment coefficient. All four encoder families (v2, v5dc, v6, vae) are thus evaluated under the same three instruments---probe recall, $k$-step per-token agreement, and across-class Pearson $r$. Full architecture, loss, and scoring details are in Appendix~\ref{app:vae-spatial}.


\section{Experiments}

We evaluate the central claim of this work---that \emph{probe accuracy is a misleading proxy for world-model usability}---with four controlled experiments. Section 4.1 describes the protocol. Sections 4.2--4.6 each target one way the claim could be wrong: perhaps probes \emph{do} track world-model quality if one averages over enough seeds (\S4.2); perhaps the gap is a peculiarity of vector quantization (\S4.3); perhaps it is driven by known codebook pathologies that a simple fix would close (\S4.4); perhaps it reflects training noise rather than a structural decoupling (\S4.5); or perhaps it disappears at larger latent scales (\S4.6). We end with two ablations that rule out cheap explanations (\S4.7) and summarize in \S4.8.

All numbers below are computed from the 26 valid checkpoints reported in Table~1: 23 DoorKey-8$\times$8 runs (v2: 5, v5: 3, v5dc: 2, v6: 5, v9: 3, vae: 5) and 3 DoorKey-16$\times$16 v6 runs. Three additional DoorKey-16$\times$16 v5dc seeds were trained but exhibited catastrophic codebook collapse on the larger 256-token grid with $K{=}512$, leaving both probe and world-model accuracy at the floor for every class; we report this finding in \S4.6 but exclude those checkpoints from the pooled correlation in \S4.5.

\subsection{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{Environments.}
We study two MiniGrid \citep{chevalier2023minigrid} tasks: DoorKey-8$\times$8 (DK-8), a 64-token grid with horizon $\approx 50$, and DoorKey-16$\times$16 (DK-16), a 256-token grid with horizon $\approx 200$. Both require picking up a key, unlocking a door, and reaching a goal. The larger grid provides both an exploration and a capacity stress test.

\paragraph{Encoders.}
We compare four encoder families, chosen to dissociate the candidate causes of the probe--WM gap (Table~1):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{v2} --- baseline VQ-VAE (codebook size $K=64$, filter width $9$). This is the plain discrete encoder used in prior MBRL work and the closest analogue to the ``discrete world model'' setting.
    \item \textbf{v5dc} --- VQ-VAE with \emph{dead-code restart} at threshold $2.0$. Dead-code restart re-initializes rarely-used codebook entries and is the standard remedy for the codebook-collapse failure mode that a low-probe score would normally flag.
    \item \textbf{v6} --- VQ-VAE with the goal-aware architecture of \cite{self_cite_prior_work}, which raises the probe substantially for task-critical classes.
    \item \textbf{vae} --- \emph{continuous spatial VAE} with no quantization, matched to v6 in backbone, decoder, and latent dimensionality. The transition model is a continuous MLP with the same hidden width and depth as the VQ transition models. This is the continuous-latent comparison for Q2.
\end{itemize}

All four encoders share the same PPO policy head and the same training recipe: 5M environment steps on DK-8 and 8M on DK-16, 4096-transition minibatches, 16 parallel envs, cosine-annealed encoder LR with snap-back regularization. The only difference between v6 and vae is the quantization layer and the corresponding loss: VQ runs use an auxiliary code-prediction cross-entropy with weight $\alpha_{\text{aux}}=0.1$; the VAE run sets $\alpha_{\text{aux}}=0$ because the loss is undefined for continuous latents. We return to this confound in \S4.7.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
For each checkpoint we record (i) \emph{policy return} as the mean best-window episode reward; (ii) \emph{per-class probe accuracy} (training-set recall) by fitting a multinomial logistic regression on frozen per-cell continuous latents drawn from a 20k-frame random-action buffer (\S3.2); (iii) \emph{per-class $k$-step WM accuracy} by rolling the transition model free-running for $k$ steps from random-action buffer states, where for VQ the predicted per-cell latent is decoded to a code index via argmax and scored by exact-match against the ground-truth index, and for the VAE the predicted per-cell continuous latent is scored by cosine-similarity argmax within the same frame (\S3.3(c)); and (iv) \emph{Pearson $r$} between probe accuracy and $k$-step WM accuracy across the five task-critical classes, per seed. The probe buffer and the WM buffer are drawn from the same random-action distribution so that the two instruments see matched states.

All numbers in \S\S4.2--4.6 are $\text{mean} \pm \text{std}$ across seeds ($N=5$ on DK-8, $N=3$ on DK-16).

\subsection{Probes and world models are decoupled (DK-8)}

\textbf{The headline result.} For every one of the four encoder families, the probe recovers task-critical classes at high accuracy (0.72--0.97 on \texttt{door}, 0.50--0.67 on \texttt{key}, 0.87--0.98 on \texttt{goal}), while the WM's one-step prediction on classes whose positions vary across episodes (\texttt{door}, \texttt{key}, \texttt{agent}) is below 0.20 for every encoder and below 0.02 for the VAE (Table~\ref{tab:perclass}, Fig.~\ref{fig:perclass_bars}). The probe--WM gap on \texttt{door} ($\Delta_{\text{door}}$) is between 0.61 and 0.96 in every row, with a near-identical pattern on \texttt{key}. The single partial exception is \texttt{goal}: in v5dc and v6 the WM reaches WM$_1 \approx 0.83$--$0.86$ on the goal token, because the goal sits in a fixed corner and can be fit as a spatial constant.

\textbf{The gap is not reducible to vector quantization.} Replacing the codebook with a continuous spatial VAE (\texttt{AEModelSpatial}; \S3.5) while retaining the convolutional backbone, MLP transition model, PPO objective, and training budget yields the \emph{largest} gap of all four families: probe accuracy on \texttt{door}, \texttt{goal} both reach 0.97, mean reward reaches 0.972, yet WM$_1$ and WM$_{10}$ are at chance ($\le 0.02$) on every class---including \texttt{goal}, where the VQ variants memorise a constant. The mechanism producing the gap must therefore survive the removal of the codebook entirely, which sharply bounds the space of candidate explanations.

\textbf{The textbook codebook fix doesn't help either.} v5dc applies dead-code restart on top of v5: rarely-used codes are re-initialised from live codes during training, which recovers rare-class probe accuracy (\texttt{probe\_door} rises from $0.638\pm0.198$ on v5 to $0.903\pm0.004$ on v5dc), without recovering WM accuracy (\texttt{WM$_1$ door} stays at $0.017\pm0.025$ on v5 and $0.012\pm0.012$ on v5dc). The dissociation survives the textbook fix for the very mechanism the probe is designed to detect (Appendix~\ref{app:full-tables}, Fig.~\ref{fig:deadcode}).

\subsection{Replication at DoorKey-16$\times$16}

The DK-8 grid is small enough that one could, in principle, argue that MLP transition models simply need more latent tokens to learn the dynamics of moving objects. DK-16 quadruples the number of tokens per frame (from 64 to 256), more than triples the episode horizon, and raises the codebook to $K=512$. We train v6 and v5dc for 3 seeds each at this scale.

At DK-16 the probe \emph{tightens} to exactly $1.00$ on \texttt{door} and \texttt{goal} for all three seeds, while the WM gap on \texttt{door}, \texttt{key}, and \texttt{agent} \emph{widens} (Fig.~\ref{fig:dk8_vs_dk16}; full table in Appendix~\ref{app:dk16-table}): WM$_1$ for \texttt{door} remains at $0.045\pm0.028$ and for \texttt{key} at $0.115\pm0.085$, each comfortably below one-tenth of its probe value. The across-class Pearson $r$ at DK-16 is remarkably stable across the three seeds---$+0.024$, $+0.027$, $+0.025$---with std effectively zero. That three independently seeded runs of a noisy stochastic system agree on the decimal point of the across-class Pearson correlation is, we think, the strongest single indicator that the dissociation is structural rather than noise-driven.

An important subtlety: the policy has not converged at DK-16 (v6 best reward $0.293\pm0.028$ after 8M steps), so the dissociation is not an artifact of over-converged policy or of a WM that has given up. The dead-code-restart variant collapses on DK-16: all three v5dc seeds converged to a near-empty effective codebook (probe and WM both at chance), leaving the per-checkpoint $r$ uninformative; we exclude these from the pooled correlation. Side-by-side bars and full numbers are in Appendix~\ref{app:dk16-v5dc} and Fig.~\ref{fig:dk8_vs_dk16}.

\subsection{Ablations and confounds}

Two specific concerns---whether the WM is evaluated out-of-distribution relative to the probe, and whether the auxiliary cross-entropy $\alpha_{\text{aux}}$ alone drives the dissociation---are addressed in Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}. In brief: probe and WM share the same random-action buffer (matched states), and the pooled correlation in \S4.5 already mixes one $\alpha_{\text{aux}}{=}0$ condition (the vae) and three $\alpha_{\text{aux}}{=}0.1$ conditions (v2, v5dc, v6), so the pooled $r$ does not depend on which we exclude. A queued ``VQ-no-aux'' control will tighten this in the camera-ready.


\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The continuous VAE provides our single cleanest datum: probe recall 0.97 on \texttt{door}, mean reward 0.97, and a world model at chance ($\le 0.02$) on every task-critical class at every horizon. Probe, policy, and value head all consume the same continuous per-cell latent; the WM consumes its discrete argmax (VQ) or the latent itself (VAE). \emph{A latent grid can simultaneously pass a linear probe on \texttt{door}, carry a policy that solves the task, and still produce a transition model that cannot predict \texttt{door} one step ahead.} The pooled bootstrap CI on the across-class Pearson correlation excludes the proxy regime decisively at every horizon and includes zero at every horizon. Where probe and WM happen to agree---the \texttt{goal} class under v5dc and v6---the agreement is a spatially-stationary token the WM fits as a constant, not a shared property between probing and propagation.

\paragraph{Implications.}
Probe accuracy should not be reported alone as evidence of representation quality in MBRL. We recommend reporting per-class $k$-step WM accuracy on a buffer drawn from the same distribution as the probe buffer, for $k \in \{1, 5, 10\}$, alongside the across-class Pearson correlation; our implementation does both in a single pass at negligible overhead. This restates a long-standing principle from value-aware model learning \citep{farahmand2018ivaml,gelada2019deepmdp}---judge the representation by the decisions it supports---in a form auditable on existing checkpoints with no retraining. The recommendation does not reject probing; probes remain a useful \emph{necessary} condition. Our framing converges with ablation-based critiques \citep{elazar2021amnesic}, world-model state-recovery metrics \citep{vafa2024evaluating}, and concurrent causal probing of IRIS/DIAMOND \citep{zhang2026probingwm} from a complementary methodological direction.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
The most informative next experiments are: (i) Transformer or RSSM transition models replacing the MLP, to test whether the structural gap is an MLP property or a representation property; (ii) richer environments (Atari, Crafter) where the per-class metrics require an alternative ground-truth vocabulary; (iii) interventions---e.g.\ a per-class auxiliary on the WM output or a value-aligned regularizer \citep{jiang2025vawmreg}---that should close the gap by construction; (iv) generalisation to settings where world-model usability is even more critical: VLA models \citep{zhang2026vlawm,molinari2025openvla}, safety-critical control with uncertainty-aware latents \citep{kolling2026evidential}, offline meta-RL \citep{nakhaeinezhadfard2026context}, self-improving world models \citep{liu2026worldverifier}; (v) richer probes (MDL, information-theoretic \citep{voita2020mdl,pimentel2020info}).

\section{Limitations}

Three limitations bound the scope of our claim. First, our experiments are confined to two MiniGrid DoorKey environments (chosen because the per-class metrics require a ground-truth spatial class vocabulary that is non-trivial in pixel-domain benchmarks such as Atari); we claim the \emph{pattern} of probe$\to$WM non-inheritance will transfer, not the specific numbers. Second, we study a single transition-model family---a flat MLP with hidden 256, depth 3---so our conclusion is an existence claim about a widespread architecture class, not a universal claim about Transformers, RSSMs, or diffusion dynamics models. Third, the probe is deliberately linear; richer probes (MLP, temporally-adjacent pairs) might correlate more tightly with WM accuracy on some classes. Extended limitations (held-out probe splits, seed-count heterogeneity, statistical power of the correlation, encoder-family coverage, scope of the negative result) are in Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}. Broader-impact discussion and compute footprint are in Appendix~\ref{app:impact}.

\section{Architecture and training objective --- full details}\label{app:arch-detail}

\paragraph{What each module consumes.}
The three consumers do not all see the same object:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l l l}
\hline
Consumer & VQ input & VAE input \\
\hline
Probe (\S3.2) & $\tilde\phi(o_t)$ (continuous $D$-dim per cell) & $\mu(o_t)$ (continuous $D$-dim per cell) \\
Policy / value & $\mathrm{vec}(\tilde\phi(o_t))$ (continuous, STE) & $\mathrm{vec}(\mu(o_t))$ (continuous) \\
WM $f_\theta$ (main loss) & code indices $z_t$ (via internal embed.) & $\mathrm{vec}(\mu(o_t))$ (continuous) \\
WM aux loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}}$ & $\mathrm{vec}(\tilde\phi(o_t))$ (continuous, STE) & (disabled) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Joint training objective.}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\omega, \psi, \theta, \eta) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}}(\eta) + \lambda_{\text{rec}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}(\omega, \psi) + \lambda_{\text{wm}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{wm}}(\omega, \theta) + \alpha_{\text{aux}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}}(\omega, \psi, \eta).
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}$ is pixel MSE (plus KL for the VAE). $\mathcal{L}_{\text{wm}}$ is the transition trainer's online one-step loss on a FIFO transition buffer: cross-entropy on $z_{t+1}$ given code indices $z_t$ for VQ; feature MSE on $\mu(o_{t+1})$ given the continuous latent for the VAE. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}}$ is a PPO-batch cross-entropy on $z_{t+1}$ predicted by $f_\theta$ from $(\mathrm{vec}(\tilde\phi(o_t)), a_t)$, undefined for continuous codes (so $\alpha_{\text{aux}}{=}0$ for the VAE). All four modules are trained jointly and on-policy; the world model is not trained to convergence on a fixed dataset but on a sliding buffer of actual transitions emitted by the current policy. This is the standard learned-latent MBRL training loop \citep{hafner2019dream,micheli2023iris,alonso2024diamond}.

\paragraph{Probe formulation.}
We fit one shared logistic regression across all token positions, $p_\beta(c \mid \tilde\phi(o)_{:,i,j}) \propto \exp(\beta_c^\top \tilde\phi(o)_{:,i,j})$, using scikit-learn with class-balanced weights ($N/(|\mathcal{C}|\, n_c)$), $C{=}1.0$, max 300 iterations. Per-class probe accuracy is recall: the count of cells with both true and predicted class $c$, divided by the count with true class $c$.

\section{Hyperparameters and reproducibility}\label{app:hparams}

\paragraph{Encoder backbone.}
All encoders share a 4-layer convolutional backbone with channel widths $(32, 64, 128, D{=}64)$, stride-2 downsampling at each layer, and ReLU activations. The filter size at the first layer is $9$ for v2 and $8$ for v5/v5dc/v6/v9/vae. Decoders are the symmetric transpose-convolutional inverse of the encoder. The VQ codebook size is $K{=}64$ for DK-8 and $K{=}512$ for DK-16; codebook vectors are 64-dim and trained with the EMA update of \citet{razavi2019vqvae2} with decay 0.99. Dead-code restart (v5dc) re-initializes any code with usage count below $\tau{=}2.0$ in the most recent transition buffer window.

\paragraph{World-model MLP.}
Hidden width 256, depth 3, ReLU, layer-norm. Action embedding 32-dim. For VQ encoders, code indices are first embedded ($K \to 64$) before being flattened and concatenated with action embedding; the output head produces $H'W' \times K$ logits decoded by argmax. For the VAE, the input and output are flat continuous vectors of dimension $D \cdot H' \cdot W'$. Same architecture across all encoder families.

\paragraph{PPO training.}
We use the standard PPO actor-critic with a single-network shared backbone (the encoder) and small policy/value heads (2-layer MLPs of width 64). Hyperparameters: 16 parallel environments, 4096-transition rollout buffer per update, 10 inner epochs, minibatch size 64, clip ratio $0.2$, entropy coefficient $0.01$, GAE $\lambda{=}0.95$, discount $\gamma{=}0.99$, learning rate $1{\times}10^{-4}$ (cosine-annealed to $0$ over training), max gradient norm $0.5$, value-function clipping enabled, normalized advantages.

\paragraph{Encoder learning rate.}
The encoder is trained with a separate learning rate $1{\times}10^{-5}$, also cosine-annealed, with a \emph{snap-back} regulariser: when the rolling-window mean reward drops by more than $0.5$ relative to its best, the encoder learning rate is briefly reset to its initial value for $100$ updates. This prevents the encoder from collapsing during PPO instability.

\paragraph{Auxiliary losses.}
Reconstruction weight $\lambda_{\text{rec}}{=}1.0$ (pixel MSE plus VQ commitment $\lambda_{\text{commit}}{=}0.25$, or VAE $\beta{=}1.0$). World-model auxiliary $\alpha_{\text{aux}}{=}0.1$ for VQ encoders, $0$ for the VAE. Semantic auxiliary $\lambda_{\text{sem}}{=}0.05$ on a 11-class classification head reading from the post-bottleneck features (focal loss with $\gamma{=}2$, class-balanced weights, hidden 128). The transition trainer runs $\mathcal{L}_{\text{wm}}$ on a FIFO buffer of the most recent $10^5$ transitions with batch size $256$.

\paragraph{Training budget.}
DK-8: 5M environment steps per run, $\approx 2$ hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090. DK-16: 8M environment steps, $\approx 9$ hours. Analysis (probe fit + 500 free-running rollouts at 4 horizons) runs in $\sim$2 minutes per checkpoint.

\paragraph{Ground-truth class extraction.}
MiniGrid frames carry an underlying object grid with integer object IDs ($\texttt{empty}{=}1$, $\texttt{wall}{=}2$, $\texttt{door}{=}4$, $\texttt{key}{=}5$, $\texttt{goal}{=}8$, $\texttt{agent}{=}10$, plus state-conditional codes). We extract this grid via the \texttt{grid.encode()} method of the underlying \texttt{minigrid.MiniGridEnv}; this label is used only for the probe and per-class WM evaluation, never as a training signal.

\paragraph{Software and seeds.}
PyTorch 2.1, gymnasium $0.29$, MiniGrid $2.3$, scikit-learn $1.3$. All randomness (env, model init, action sampling, dataloader) is seeded with the run seed. Code at \texttt{[anonymized URL]}; per-checkpoint analysis JSONs and the script that produced every figure in this paper (\texttt{paper\_assets/make\_figures.py}) are released alongside.

\section{Probe evaluation protocol --- full details}\label{app:probe-protocol}

We do not hold out a test split. Linear probes with $O(10^3)$ parameters on $O(10^6)$ samples are far from overfitting in the generalization-gap sense, so the training-set recall is within a few percentage points of a held-out recall on this data; but the reported number is nonetheless an upper bound on probe generalization. If the probe were hand-tuned downward by a held-out split, the gap between Probe$_c$ and WM$_c^{(k)}$ would only widen.

The buffer is drawn from random actions, which biases it toward states near episode start (agent exploring near the spawn) and away from states that require key pickup to reach. This makes \texttt{door}, \texttt{key}, and \texttt{goal} instances rarer in the buffer than under the trained policy, but not absent: the 16-env parallel walk at horizon $\approx 50$ still exposes doors and keys in every episode. The WM evaluation uses the same random-action distribution, so the probe and the WM are measured on matched states.

Recall (rather than precision or $F_1$) is reported because the dissociation we study concerns \emph{retrieving} task-critical classes from the latent, and recall is the most direct per-class measure of this.

\section{World-model accuracy protocol --- full details}\label{app:wm-protocol}

\paragraph{Bucketing alternative.}
An alternative metric that buckets cells by class at step $k$ rather than at $t{=}0$ is reasonable too, but conflates the WM's ability to track existing class-$c$ cells with its ability to \emph{generate new} class-$c$ cells (e.g.\ a door disappearing or an agent teleporting into a position). We avoid that conflation and bucket by the ground-truth class at $t{=}0$ (\texttt{sem\_labels\_0} in \texttt{analyze\_wm\_multistep.py:273}).

\paragraph{Free-running rollout under random actions.}
We feed the WM's own output back as input at every step and use the same random-action policy that generated the probe buffer. This matches the conditions under which a planner would query the WM and uses the same state distribution as the probe.

\paragraph{Per-cell match, not feature MSE.}
For the continuous VAE the WM's output is a real-valued vector. We use a \textbf{cosine-similarity argmax within a frame}: a predicted token at position $(i,j)$ is correct iff, among all $H'W'$ tokens in the same frame, the one whose normalized embedding has the highest cosine similarity to $\hat\ell_{k, ij}$ is the one at position $(i, j)$ in the true encoding. This avoids introducing a post-hoc quantizer (a free parameter that would bias the comparison) and is strictly stronger than continuous MSE.

\section{AEModelSpatial: full architecture and loss}\label{app:vae-spatial}

Given the encoder output $\phi_\omega(o) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times H' \times W'}$, \texttt{AEModelSpatial} applies two $1 \times 1$ convolutions:
\begin{equation}
\mu(o) = \mathrm{Conv}_{1\times 1}^\mu\big(\phi_\omega(o)\big), \qquad
\log\sigma(o) = \mathrm{Conv}_{1\times 1}^\sigma\big(\phi_\omega(o)\big), \qquad
\mu, \log\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times H' \times W'}.
\end{equation}
The latent is $z(o) = \mu(o) + \sigma(o) \odot \epsilon$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$; at inference we use $\mu$. For use by the flat MLP transition model and the PPO policy head, $z$ is flattened to $\mathbb{R}^{D \cdot H' \cdot W'}$; the original spatial structure is recovered by reshape whenever per-cell access is needed.

\texttt{AEModelSpatial} is trained under a standard $\beta$-VAE objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}(\omega, \psi) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q_\omega}[\|\mathrm{dec}(z) - o\|^2] + \beta \cdot D_{\text{KL}}\big(q_\omega(z \mid o) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, I)\big),
\end{equation}
with $\beta$ matched to the default VQ commitment coefficient. The decoder, policy head, and transition MLP are otherwise identical to v6.

The continuous-latent scoring rule applies the cosine-similarity argmax within a frame directly to the per-cell slices of the flat latent, with no auxiliary codebook. This keeps the evaluation protocol as close as possible to the VQ case.

\section{Ablations and confounds}\label{app:ablations}

\paragraph{(1) Buffer distribution match.}
The probe and the WM evaluation are drawn from the \emph{same random-action rollout distribution} (fresh \texttt{env.reset()}, uniform actions). The WM is therefore not being evaluated out-of-distribution relative to the probe: both instruments see the same marginal state distribution, and the WM is asked to propagate classes on exactly the kind of state the probe successfully classifies.

\paragraph{(2) The $\alpha_{\text{aux}}$ confound.}
The VAE condition differs from the VQ conditions in two ways---the quantization layer and the auxiliary cross-entropy weight. To separate the two, a ``VQ-no-aux'' control trains v6 with $\alpha_{\text{aux}}=0$ for 3 seeds on DK-8. This run is queued but has not yet been launched at submission; we report the result in the final version. The prediction from \S4.3 is that VQ-no-aux matches vanilla v6 in both probe and WM accuracy, since the auxiliary loss is secondary to the transition-trainer gradient on the VQ transition model. Even if VQ-no-aux were to differ from v6, the dissociation in \S4.5 already pools across one $\alpha_{\text{aux}}{=}0.0$ condition (vae) and three $\alpha_{\text{aux}}{=}0.1$ conditions (v2, v5dc, v6), and the pooled $r$ does not depend on which we exclude.

\paragraph{Training-time WM accuracy.}
Training-time WM accuracy on \texttt{door} and \texttt{key} plateaus at the same values we observe at eval, ruling out underfitting to the training schedule; per-code hit-rate statistics show no systematic bias against task-critical codes beyond what dead-code restart (v5dc) already corrects.

\section{Extended limitations}\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{Probe evaluation on training set.}
Our probe reports training-set recall. Under a proper held-out split the probe numbers would move at most a few percentage points on $10^6$-sample fits, and such a shift could only \emph{increase} the probe--WM gap we report. The camera-ready version will include held-out-split numbers.

\paragraph{Seed counts are heterogeneous across encoder families.}
The four main DK-8 encoder families have between 2 (v5dc) and 5 (v2, v6, vae) seeds, and the auxiliary v5/v9 controls have 3 each. The pooled bootstrap CI in Table~4 weighs each checkpoint equally and is therefore dominated by the families with more seeds, but the per-family rows show that no family individually yields a CI excluding the proxy null---the conclusion does not depend on which family one weights more heavily.

\paragraph{Statistical power of the correlation.}
The per-seed Pearson correlation is taken across five class-level observations per seed. Any Pearson $r$ computed from $n{=}5$ points is inherently noisy per-seed; the bootstrap CIs we report reflect this. Point estimates (all near zero) are meaningful in aggregate; claims about the sign or magnitude of the correlation for any single seed should not be drawn from these tables.

\paragraph{Encoder family.}
All four encoders share a common convolutional backbone; only the bottleneck differs. ViT, perceiver, and state-space encoders are untested. The continuous-latent VAE comparison addresses only the most natural alternative bottleneck and does not exhaust the design space.

\paragraph{Scope of the negative result.}
We show that probe accuracy is not a reliable proxy for world-model usability in the regime we study. We do not show that the two quantities are \emph{inherently} incomparable, nor do we show that no probe-based surrogate for WM accuracy exists. A richer probe---e.g.\ a two-layer MLP, or a probe trained on temporally-adjacent latent pairs---might correlate more tightly with the WM, and we view characterizing such probes as a useful next step rather than as a refutation of our findings.

\section{Broader impact and compute footprint}\label{app:impact}

This work is methodological. Our direct contribution is a critique of a widespread evaluation shortcut and a drop-in replacement for it; we neither introduce new environments, new RL agents, nor new model architectures that could be deployed in the world. The foreseeable impact is therefore on \emph{reporting practice} in the model-based RL research community rather than on deployed systems.

\paragraph{Intended positive impact.}
Probe-only evaluations risk falsely certifying an MBRL system as ``representing'' task-critical structure when the downstream world model is in fact unable to use that structure. In safety-adjacent applications of MBRL---dexterous manipulation, autonomous driving, clinical decision support---a world model that is probe-certified but semantically useless will silently produce over-confident rollouts. Our recommended joint probe + WM metric, at near-zero marginal cost over a probe-only analysis, reduces the chance that a fragile world model passes an internal evaluation that it should not.

\paragraph{Risk of over-correction.}
A second-order worry is that a published critique of probing leads practitioners to abandon probes entirely, losing a cheap diagnostic that is genuinely useful for detecting what a representation does \emph{not} encode. We frame our recommendation to mitigate this: probes are necessary but not sufficient, and should be reported alongside---not replaced by---world-model metrics.

\paragraph{Research-ecosystem risk.}
Strengthening an evaluation standard always raises the cost of publication. We mitigate this by (i) providing an open-source implementation of the joint evaluation in \texttt{analyze\_wm\_multistep.py}, callable in a single command on an existing checkpoint, with runtime dominated by the probe fit rather than by the WM rollout; and (ii) explicitly recommending that existing probe-only papers in MBRL be re-scored rather than re-trained.

\paragraph{Compute footprint.}
The full experimental sweep reported in this paper consumes approximately 110 GPU-hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090: about 50 hours for the 23 DK-8 training runs ($\approx 2$~h each) and about 60 hours for the 6 DK-16 runs ($\approx 9$~h each, due to the 8M-step horizon). All analysis (probes and multi-step WM rollouts) runs in under one hour of total GPU time across all 26 reported checkpoints.
